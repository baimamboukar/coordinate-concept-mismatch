The project asks whether frozen-probe transfer failure is caused by incompatible coordinates or by deeper representational differences.

Exact residual permutations first established sufficiency: behavior was preserved, naive probe transfer failed, and analytic transport restored performance. MLP-neuron and grouped-query-attention permutations reproduced this result in modern models, showing that planted parameter symmetries can break probes without changing the model function.

Natural Pythia alignment recovered a median 60.3% of the gap with permutation-diagonal maps and about 97% with flexible maps. Across Llama, Qwen, Mistral, Granite, and Nemotron, all 20 cross-family comparisons failed; restricted maps recovered a median 61.8%, flexible maps 97.3%, and shuffled maps none. Flexible alignment shows linear recoverability, not exact symmetry.

Positive-diagonal controls then isolated transformation geometry and magnitude. Weak scaling caused little damage, while stronger scaling produced monotonic gaps concentrated in degree-2 probes. The same probe-family sensitivity replicated on SST-2, establishing that fragility depends on the symmetry magnitude, probe architecture, and task.

The OLMo 2 baseline supplied the missing modern same-architecture bridge. Stage-2 seed pairs produced no primary late-layer failure, but SST-2 exposed early-layer failures that flexible maps—not restricted ones—could recover. This localized the strongest remaining contrast to Stage-1→Stage-2 transfer at 75% depth.

The prespecified stage-transition follow-up then found substantial permutation-plus-diagonal recovery in all six eligible late-layer failures: median recovery was 77.0%, and the mean oracle gap fell from 0.165 to 0.040. Exact permutation recovered only 1/6, flexible maps recovered 6/6, and shuffled alignment recovered none. Recovery was absent under the restricted map at earlier depths and slightly harmed reverse directions that had not failed. Low-FPR detection and calibration improved but remained below the target-trained oracle.

The evidence therefore supports a qualified conclusion: coordinate mismatch is sufficient to cause probe failure and explains a substantial component of this natural stage transition, but natural failure is task-, depth-, and direction-dependent. Learned activation alignment is not proof of exact parameter symmetry. Next, test independently initialized same-architecture checkpoints and replicate the stage-transition result on a second task or model family.
