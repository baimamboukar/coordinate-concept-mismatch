The project asks whether frozen-probe transfer failure is caused by incompatible coordinates or by deeper representational differences.

Exact residual, MLP-neuron, and attention-component permutations first established sufficiency: behavior was preserved, naive transfer failed, and analytic transport restored the probes. Planted parameter symmetries can therefore cause transfer failure without changing the model function.

Natural Pythia alignment recovered 60.3% of the median gap with restricted maps and about 97% with flexible maps. Across Llama, Qwen, Mistral, Granite, and Nemotron, all 20 cross-family comparisons failed; corresponding recovery was 61.8% and 97.3%, while shuffled maps recovered none. Flexible alignment shows recoverability, not exact symmetry.

Positive-diagonal controls then isolated transformation geometry and magnitude. Weak scaling caused little damage, while stronger scaling produced monotonic gaps concentrated in degree-2 probes. The same probe-family sensitivity replicated on SST-2, establishing that fragility depends on the symmetry magnitude, probe architecture, and task.

OLMo 2 then supplied a modern same-architecture bridge. Stage-2 seed pairs had no primary late-layer failure, but Stage-1→Stage-2 transfer yielded six eligible failures. Permutation-plus-diagonal alignment recovered all six, with 77.0% median recovery and a mean oracle gap reduction from 0.165 to 0.040. The effect disappeared at earlier depths and in reverse directions that had not failed.

The independent-training test compared architecture-identical Ai2 and AMD OLMo 1B checkpoints under shared tokenization. All four primary transfers failed on both SST-2 and WildGuardMix, with median AUROC gaps of 0.406 and 0.461. Restricted recovery was only 31.9% and 44.3%, passing in 1/4 comparisons on each task. Orthogonal recovery reached 92.5% and 90.5%; affine and quotient maps reached 99.1% and 94.9%, passing all comparisons. Shuffled maps passed none. Dense alignment restored low-FPR detection and calibration near the target-trained oracle on both tasks, while restricted outcomes remained depth-, direction-, and probe-dependent.

Together, the experiments show that coordinate mismatch is sufficient and can explain substantial natural failures, but exact symmetry-like recovery is not universal. Dense recovery identifies a shared linearly recoverable task signal, not a parameter symmetry. Next, fit each model-to-model map on one task and evaluate it on the other without refitting; task-independent transport would distinguish a global coordinate change from task-local activation matching.
